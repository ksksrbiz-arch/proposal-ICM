# Design standard and rejection tests

The business should be recognizable before the logo is read. A premium proposal is specific, useful and carefully finished. It is not a collection of fashionable components.

## What must be authored

| Dimension | Required evidence | Reject when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business identity | Matched facts, original voice, local/customer context | Wrong city, copied biography, invented owner or generic promises |
| Opening | Asset-led composition and one clear primary action | Huge empty hero, vague headline, floating cards unrelated to the business |
| Photography | Authentic subject, provenance, intentional crop, complete-image access where useful | Stock people standing in for staff, fake before/after, cropped-off tattoo/garment details |
| Typography | Deliberate family/scale/line length, useful contrast between display and body | Tiny phone copy, decorative illegibility, same font/layout as every sibling site |
| Page architecture | Substantial distinct routes mapped to actual customer decisions | One long homepage with pretend navigation; thin pages added to inflate count |
| Journey | Real call/booking/menu/portfolio/visit outcome | Fake form success, dead buttons, invented inventory or booking availability |
| Finish | Balanced whitespace, readable density, polished states, keyboard behavior | Unbounded blank space, overlapping sticky actions, filler sections, inert controls |

## Voice

Use concrete services, locations and useful preparation. Prefer “Bring the shoes you’ll wear with the dress” to “Experience unparalleled craftsmanship.” Delete unsupported superlatives, “elevate,” “unlock,” “seamless solutions,” generic “why choose us” claims and filler FAQ questions. A business's genuine quoted voice can differ; preserve attribution rather than rewriting it into an AI slogan.

Do not promise sales, rankings, guaranteed results or future bookings. Do not call an independent proposal their official site. Do not invent live Google reviews; date the observed rating/review snapshot and use a verified source link. Do not pull private family details from old press into marketing copy.

## Art direction review before full build

Provide a real asset contact sheet, a mobile and desktop composition sketch/screenshot, final hero wording and one representative secondary-page plan. Root checks business relevance and batch diversity. Choose design decisions from the business: a counter service board, complete bridal garment photographs, a real roadside stand, paired landscaping originals, a color-work lookbook, or a tattoo portfolio. These are examples of reasoning, not mandatory templates.

For sparse assets, typography, layout and honest graphic treatment must carry the experience. Avoid installing a generic gallery with unverified photos just to satisfy a checklist. Generated diagrams, textures or proposed mascot art must be purposeful and explicitly proposed. Preserve existing logos and mascots when instructed; “revamp” is not permission to erase identity.

## Useful interaction test

For each feature answer: who uses it, what decision it helps, why the simpler option is inadequate, and how failure works. If those answers are weak, omit it. Motion must explain state or support navigation; it must not delay content or fight reduced motion. Local preparation tools are optional and must clearly say nothing has been submitted.

## Review rubric

0 missing, 1 poor, 2 generic/incomplete, 3 competent but obvious defects, 4 polished and business-specific, 5 unusually strong with precise supporting evidence. Score specificity, art direction, copy/content, mobile usability, customer journeys and finish/accessibility. Each must reach 4; total at least 26. No critical/high defects. A good score does not waive facts, source provenance, privacy, meaningful coverage or functioning primary actions.

Every route needs independent mobile and desktop inspection. Include above-fold, middle, footer, menu/dialog open and key flow states. Compare the weakest page with the strongest. A page count is only a coverage floor; the review must name concrete improvements over the current site's composition, content, readability, workflow or performance and preserve what it already does well.
